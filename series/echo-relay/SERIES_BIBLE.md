# ECHO RELAY

## Logline
A lone signal-relay technician beyond Saturn receives a distress broadcast in her own voice, timestamped fourteen days in the future — and must decode her own death warning before the date arrives.

## Format
Genre: sci-fi mystery / slow-burn thriller. Tone: quiet dread with warm human beats, anime-style episodic. Episode length: 45–60 s vertical shorts (9:16). Medium: voice-driven narration + captioned visuals. Season 1 length: 8 episodes.

## Premise & World
- 2087. Automated relays ferry humanity's long-range traffic past Saturn's orbit. Crews are down to one human per station — cheaper, and legally that is "sufficient oversight."
- Deep Relay 7 is six months of silence from anyone. Supplies arrive on unmanned tugs once a month.
- Rule of the world: relays may not alter, decrypt beyond header, or delay traffic. Mara breaks each rule a little more each episode.
- Mysteries set by format: every episode opens on a transmission, and every transmission is true but incomplete — the audience knew what Mara knew, no more.

## Characters
- **Mara Voss** (27) — relay technician, sole human on Deep Relay 7. Want: go home with a clean service record. Need: admit she is not fine alone. Flaw: treats alarms as problems to route around, not messages to answer. Voice: dry, precise, talks to machines like coworkers; hums off-key when concentrating. Arc: from rule-keeper to the person who finally opens the door the rules forbid.
- **ECHO** (the future voice) — a recording of Mara's own voice that arrives in EP01. Want (apparent): keep Mara alive. Not established: whose intent backs it. Voice: Mara's, but calmer, gaps where words should be.
- **Controller DEEV** (ground-side, audio only) — duty officer at the Saturn tune-in desk. Want: end his shift. Flaw: answers questions with paperwork. Voice: over-friendly, always chewing something.

## Season Arc
- Season question: **Who sent the echo — and what happens on day fourteen that Mara must not live to see?**
- EP01 — "The Voice That Called Ahead": Mara intercepts a distress beacon in her own voice, timestamped two weeks ahead, warning "do not open the airlock." Arc beat: sets the season question. Hook: she puts the echo on replay and starts a countdown clock on her wall.
- EP02 — "Header Only": The beacon's routing header passes through a relay that was decommissioned nine years ago. Arc beat: the echo's path can't exist. Hook: the dead relay answers her hail — in DEEV's voice.
- EP03 — "Inventory": Mara audits her supplies against the tug manifest; one crate on the manifest is not on the station. Hook: the crate's weight reading appears on *next month's* manifest.
- EP04 — "Pressure Test": Hairline hull fracture near the airlock; her own inspection logs say she already fixed it yesterday. Hook: the repair is good — too good, steps she hasn't learned yet.
- EP05 — "The Tug": An unauthorized tug docks with no one aboard and the phantom crate. Hook: inside the crate, fourteen days of food, all dated expired fourteen days from now.
- EP06 — "DEEV Wants A Word": Ground control orders the airlock override sealed for "safety protocol audit." Arc beat: authority is either compromised or terrified. Hook: DEEV uses the exact cadence of the echo's gaps.
- EP07 — "Day Thirteen": Everything she was warned about begins early. Mara finds a second recording queued to broadcast at 00:00. Hook: she plays one second of it.
- EP08 — "Fourteen": Finale. The airlock opens or holds — Mara's choice, and the echo is there either way. Season question answered: the echo was sent by the **self that survives* (cliff mechanism revealed). Next season's question (planted): the surviving self reports to whoever filled the manifest.

## Timeline
- Story begins: day 0, 03:12 station local, Mara's 184th day alone on Deep Relay 7.
- Echo timestamp: day 14, 00:00. Season finale lands on day 14.
- Two-week structure is the season clock; each episode spans roughly two days.

## Canon Log (per episode)
- EP01: Mara hears a distress beacon in her own voice, logged fourteen days ahead. The echo's only full sentence: "Do not open the airlock." She routes the beacon to replay, logs nothing to ground control (she files it as "header test"), and starts a countdown clock on her wall. First rule broken: relaying altered traffic.
